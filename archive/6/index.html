Treasury license would let Russian diesel flow through April 2027

The diesel deal's sanctions carve-out reportedly extends through April 2027 — turning a wartime fix into a year-plus arrangement.

Updated 27 Oct 2026, 03:34 UTC Verified 14 sources
Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/treasury-license-russian-diesel/

What happened

This is a developing-story monitor. The thread: the Treasury license underpinning the Trump–Putin diesel arrangement reportedly runs through April 2027 — well past the US midterms and deep into the war's expected runway. Awaiting verification: the license text itself.

What is verified

  • OFAC general license authorizes Russian-diesel sale/delivery/offloading/import through 12:01 am EDT, April 7, 2027 (The Hindu/PTI, Oct 10, 2026).
  • The license excludes debits to accounts of Russia's central bank, national wealth fund, and finance ministry at US institutions (The National, Oct 9, 2026).
  • Trump described delivery phases totaling ~4.8M tons nominal (Truth Social via The Hindu/PTI/USA Today; exact phasing varies across outlets).
  • The diesel deal includes a temporary US sanctions lift on Russian fuel (Haaretz, Oct 10, 2026).
  • Trump signed a Section 115 waiver of the Graham Act duties/sanctions Oct 16-17 Washington time, just as the 30-day implementation deadline expired; order text certifies the waiver 'is in the national interests of the United States' (SCMP/Bloomberg/ANI/Tribune/TOI, Oct 17 — VERIFIED)
  • Waived: duties under Sections 112 and 113 (up to 500% ad valorem on Russian goods, up to 100% on five largest importers of Russian crude/gas); sanctions/restrictions under Section 102(d) and other provisions (TOI — VERIFIED); the levies remain formally imposed, so Trump could re-apply them (SCMP — attributed legal analysis)
  • Secretary of State Marco Rubio said President Trump would use up to 60 days to assess the economic consequences of the waiver on key sanctions and tariffs under the Graham Act (Reuters via Times of India / Economic Times / Straits Times, Oct 19 — on-record statement)
  • Trump said Russia would immediately supply more than 300,000 tonnes of diesel to the US and global markets, followed by 500,000 tonnes in November and 1 million tonnes after that, plus a further 3 million tonnes depending on the condition of Russian refineries (Oil & Gas Journal — reported, attributed)
  • Delivery remains uncertain: Ukrainian strikes have damaged Russian refineries, and Moscow's diesel export restrictions remain partly in place despite easing on October 10 (same — attributed)
  • Deputy PM Alexander Novak said Wednesday the first tanker carrying 40,000 metric tons of Russian diesel for the US departs Thursday, October 22; fuel paperwork nearing completion; further shipments to follow (Vedomosti/RIA via The Hindu BusinessLine and Ukrainska Pravda, Oct 21 — on-record statement, VERIFIED)
  • European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said Wednesday morning the EU will not ease sanctions on Russian fossil fuels despite the US diesel deal (Politico, Oct 21 — on-record remarks, VERIFIED)
  • Dmitriev (Oct 22): first 40,000-ton diesel tanker sailed Thursday for the US under the Trump-Putin deal — first Russian fuel shipment to the US since 2022 (VERIFIED as his announcement; independent sailing confirmation not yet in hand) (The USA New Times).
  • Bipartisan Senate/House letters sent Saturday warn the CEOs of six major banks and the heads of the Houston, Corpus Christi, Beaumont and New York/New Jersey port authorities that facilitating Russian-diesel imports may expose them to criminal and civil liability under 18 U.S.C. \u00a7 545 and/or 19 U.S.C. \u00a7 1595a, demand preservation of all related records and a written confirmation by October 28, 2026 (Senate Foreign Relations Committee release, Oct 26 — VERIFIED as the letters; IANS, Oct 27, second publisher). The letters' legal reading is attributed, not a court finding; no administration, bank or port response appears in the material reviewed.

What remains disputed

The exact expiry date, the scope of products covered, renewal terms, and whether Congress or allies were consulted. An 18-month carve-out is a different animal than a 90-day emergency waiver — the difference matters. The legal validity of OFAC General License No. 135 is now openly contested: House Judiciary Ranking Member Raskin argues (Oct 26) it cannot override the 2022 statutory ban; no court ruling on the license's validity appears in the material reviewed.

Why it matters

Duration is the story. A short waiver is crisis management; a license through April 2027 is a de facto sanctions regime change for Russian refined products. It would give Moscow a year-plus revenue channel and give US fuel markets a year-plus of Russian supply — with everything that implies for Ukraine policy and European coordination.

Strategic context

US sanctions on Russian energy have been the West's central economic weapon since 2022. A long-dated license punches a hole in that regime — and hands Moscow leverage: every renewal date becomes a negotiation.

Competing interpretations

Reading one: pragmatic — markets need certainty, and a long license prevents a price cliff. Reading two: this is normalization by the back door, executed without a public policy debate. Reading three: the date may be a ceiling, not a commitment — licenses can be revoked.

TUESDAY — BIPARTISAN SENATE/HOUSE LETTERS WARN BANKS AND PORTS AGAINST FACILITATING RUSSIAN DIESEL IMPORTS — Senators Jeanne Shaheen (Ranking Member, Senate Foreign Relations Committee), Ron Wyden, Sheldon Whitehouse, Chris Coons and Elizabeth Warren, Republican Thom Tillis, and Representative Gregory W. Meeks (Ranking Member, House Foreign Affairs Committee) sent letters on Saturday to the CEOs of JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, Citigroup, Wells Fargo Bank, Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley and to the heads of the port authorities of Houston, Corpus Christi and Beaumont in Texas and of New York/New Jersey, warning that importing Russian diesel raises significant legal questions and may be subject to legal action and congressional investigations, with criminal and civil liability under 18 U.S.C. \u00a7 545 and/or 19 U.S.C. \u00a7 1595a — including for a company that knowingly buys Russian diesel or facilitates its importation, not simply the importer of record (Senate Foreign Relations Committee release, Oct 26 — VERIFIED as the letters' existence and contents; IANS reported the letters Oct 27, a second publisher, not an independent confirmation of the letter text). The letters cite the 2022 Ending Importation of Russian Oil Act's general bar on Russian-origin energy imports unless the President, after consultation with Congress, certifies Russia has ceased hostilities in Ukraine, poses no immediate military threat to NATO members and recognises Ukrainians' right to choose their government, and cite CAATSA Section 216's congressional-review requirement, which the letters say the administration has not fulfilled; they demand recipients preserve all records relating to any proposed or actual Russian-diesel importation and confirm in writing by October 28, 2026 whether they have been involved in or have knowledge of any such transaction. The release also says public reporting and vessel-tracking data indicate ships laden with Russian diesel are on track to deliver to Beaumont and New York/New Jersey as soon as the end of this month — ATTRIBUTED to the release's cited reporting/tracking, not an independently verified arrival, and no arrival is claimed here. MONDAY — RASKIN WARNS OIL INDUSTRY OF CRIMINAL EXPOSURE OVER RUSSIAN DIESEL — House Judiciary Ranking Member Jamie Raskin wrote on October 26 to the American Petroleum Institute, the US Chamber of Commerce and the American Fuel & Petrochemical Manufacturers, warning that companies helping sell Russian diesel in the US could face civil penalties of up to twice the transaction value and criminal penalties of up to $1 million and 20 years in prison (House Judiciary Committee Democrats press release, Oct 26 — VERIFIED as Raskin's letter and its contents as published by the Committee). Raskin's argument: the 2022 Ending Importation of Russian Oil Act requires the President to consult Congress and certify conditions — including an agreement accepted by Ukraine's freely elected government to withdraw Russian forces and cease hostilities — before the ban can end, and the administration did not follow those procedures before OFAC issued General License No. 135, so the license 'provides no shield' from civil or criminal liability. STATUS: ATTRIBUTED — Raskin's legal reading is contested ground, not an adopted legal finding; no administration response and no court ruling on GL 135's validity appears in the material reviewed. PLACEMENT: legal-risk overhang on this stack's license thread; changes no shipment, volume or license-date item. Raskin's reference to the October 16 waiver of the Graham Act sanctions layer is consistent with this stack's v3. DEPARTURE CONFIRMED (ATTRIBUTED) — Putin's investment envoy Kirill Dmitriev says the first 40,000-metric-ton diesel tanker sailed for the US on Thursday, October 22, under the Trump-Putin deal — the first Russian fuel shipment to the US since 2022 (The USA New Times, Oct 22 — VERIFIED as Dmitriev's announcement; independent sailing confirmation not yet in hand). The vessel's port of departure and the destination US port were still undisclosed at press time. Full ledger at st-001 v80. FIRST CARGO THURSDAY — Deputy PM Alexander Novak said Wednesday that the first tanker carrying 40,000 metric tons of Russian diesel for the US departs on Thursday, October 22, with the fuel paperwork nearing completion and further shipments to follow (Vedomosti/RIA via The Hindu BusinessLine and Ukrainska Pravda, Oct 21 — VERIFIED as Novak's on-record statement). EU SPLIT — Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said Wednesday morning the EU will not ease Russian energy sanctions despite the US diesel deal (Politico, Oct 21 — VERIFIED as her remarks). Full ledger at st-001 v78. BESSENT — 'NO SANCTIONS WERE LIFTED ON FRIDAY' — Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Sunday on NBC's Meet the Press (Oct 18) that the Graham Act waiver was a 60-day review, not a rollback: 'No sanctions were lifted on Friday' (NBC via The HinduBusinessLine, Oct 20 — VERIFIED as Bessent's on-record remarks; single carrying chain at build time). Bessent revealed the US had asked its European allies to impose secondary sanctions on China and India for buying Russian oil and they refused — 'why should the US go it alone' — and noted European countries had waived their own embargo on purchases of Russian diesel and jet fuel back in May (same). Adds Treasury's framing of the v5/v6 60-day evaluation window and exposes the transatlantic burden-sharing tension behind the waiver. Cross-linked to st-001 (diesel deal). DIESEL DEAL VOLUMES — Trump said Russia would immediately supply more than 300,000 tonnes of diesel to the US and global markets, followed by 500,000 tonnes in November and 1 million tonnes after that, plus a further 3 million tonnes depending on the condition of Russian refineries (Oil & Gas Journal — reported, attributed); delivery remains uncertain — Ukrainian strikes have damaged Russian refineries and Moscow's diesel export restrictions remain partly in place despite easing on October 10 (same — attributed). Cross-linked to st-001. RUBIO: INDIA TARIFFS DEFERRED, NOT CANCELLED — Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the US is 'very close' to a trade deal with India and that global energy needs weighed in the Graham Act waiver: India is 'still getting a substantial amount of energy from Russia because it's hard to pivot from one day to the next' (Hindustan Times, Oct 19 13:08 IST — VERIFIED as Rubio's on-record remarks; single-carrier at build time). Rubio noted the tariffs have been deferred, not permanently cancelled, and the law remains in place — so the waiver offers India only temporary certainty; India says the first tranche of the bilateral trade agreement is finalised and undergoing legal drafting (same). Adds the India-specific mechanics behind v5's 60-day evaluation window. Cross-linked to st-001 (diesel deal). RUBIO: 60-DAY EVALUATION WINDOW — Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Trump would use up to 60 days to assess the economic consequences of the Graham Act waiver that unlocked Russian diesel exports (Reuters via Times of India / Economic Times / Straits Times, Oct 19 — on-record statement from Rubio, carried on the wire). The waiver followed Trump's deal with Putin for Russian diesel shipments to the US and EU markets; Brent slipped to ~$108.4 on the repricing (see st-001 v66). The license's formal horizon stays April 7, 2027 (v2). Cross-linked to st-001 (diesel deal) and st-008 (reserve-release thread). GRAHAM ACT SECTION 115 WAIVER SIGNED — On Oct 16-17 Washington time, Trump used Section 115 of the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act to waive the law's duties and sanctions just as its 30-day implementation deadline was expiring. Verbatim from the order text (via ANI): 'I have submitted to the Congress a certification that the issuance of such a waiver is in the national interests of the United States and a report explaining the basis for the certification, consistent with section 115(b) of the Graham Act' — Section 115 'authorises the President to waive the application of any sanctions provision with respect to a foreign person, any restriction with respect to a person, and any duty under the law.' Waived: duties under Sections 112 and 113 (up to 500% ad valorem on Russian goods, up to 100% on the five largest importers of Russian crude/gas); sanctions/restrictions under Section 102(d) and 'several other provisions covering sanctions against foreign persons and restrictions on individuals' (TOI/SCMP/Bloomberg, Oct 17 — VERIFIED). Crucially, the directive 'does formally impose the levies, meaning that Trump could apply them at another time if he chooses' (SCMP — ATTRIBUTED as legal analysis) — the levies are formally imposed but suspended, so the President retains re-imposition optionality while clearing the legal path for the Russian diesel flows GL 135 already licensed. The order was posted on the White House website Friday evening US time. NEGATIVE FINDINGS — no OFAC amendment to GL 135, no new FAQ/guidance, no confirmed first cargo at a US port, no named US refiner or charter fixture, no European/Canadian license-mechanics response in the last 7 days. Adjacent (already in cycle): bipartisan senators' letter demanding rescission (reported Oct 13, not new); analyst arithmetic on the 300,000-ton tranche (~13.5-14.8 hours of US distillate demand) from Oct 10-12 commentary, not new developments. Cross-linked to st-001 (diesel deal politics). GRAHAM ACT WAIVER — Trump signed an executive order waiving the application of the sanctions, restrictions and duties in the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 (100%-duty authority over major buyers of Russian oil and gas), certifying to Congress that the waiver is in US national interests; the full verified ledger is on st-001 (multi-outlet). This is a separate legal instrument from the OFAC diesel general license through April 7, 2027 already on this stack — the waiver clears the newly legislated sanctions layer; the OFAC carve-out governs the diesel transactions themselves. THE LICENSE TEXT IS OUT — OFAC's general license authorizes all transactions prohibited by the Russian Harmful Foreign Activities Sanctions Regulations related to the sale, delivery, offloading or importation (including into the US) of Russian-origin diesel through 12:01 am EDT, April 7, 2027 (The Hindu/PTI, Oct 10). The license does NOT authorize debits to accounts of Russia's central bank, national wealth fund or finance ministry at US financial institutions (The National, Oct 9). Trump on Truth Social described phases — 300,000+ tons immediately, 500,000 tons in November, 1 million tons immediately thereafter, then 3 million tons more conditional on refinery condition (~4.8M tons nominal total; USA Today reports the November tranche as 1M tons — phasing differs across outlets and is treated as reported, not settled). monitor opened on the license-duration thread.

What to watch next

  • Whether the administration or any oil company responds to Raskin's warning, and whether any court challenge to GL 135 materializes.
  • Publication of the actual Treasury/OFAC license text.
  • European reaction — does Brussels follow or object?
  • Ukrainian government response.
  • Whether the license covers only diesel or wider refined products.
  • Whether Brussels objects to or mirrors the carve-out for Russian refined products.

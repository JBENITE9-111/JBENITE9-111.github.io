US sanctions the entire ICC — Rubio vows to dismantle it 'brick by brick'

The US sanctioned the Hague court as an institution on Oct 9, hours after a former ICC judge won the Nobel Peace Prize — demanding the Netanyahu/Gallant warrants be dropped and issuing an April 2027 ultimatum. Canada, France, Germany, Japan and the UK rejected the move.

Updated 24 Oct 2026, 17:21 UTC Verified 24 sources
Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/rubio-icc-sanctions/

What happened

This is a developing-story monitor. The thread: the US, with Secretary of State Marco Rubio as the public face, is moving to sanction the International Criminal Court. Awaiting verification: the specific designations, the legal instrument used, and the stated rationale.

What is verified

  • The US sanctioned the ICC as an institution on Oct 9 — the first time the entire tribunal was sanctioned (AP/CNA/The National, Oct 9-10, 2026).
  • Rubio announced the move and demanded: cancel Netanyahu/Gallant warrants, close the Afghanistan/CIA investigation, reform the Rome Statute (Le Monde, Oct 10, 2026).
  • Sanctions take full effect by April 9, 2027 unless the court complies; a six-month wind-down grace period applies (AP/Le Monde/CNA, Oct 9-10, 2026).
  • The announcement came hours after former ICC judge Navi Pillay won the Nobel Peace Prize (CNA, USA Today, Oct 9-10, 2026).
  • The ICC rejected the sanctions ('history will judge this moment'); President Akane Tomoko called them 'historically unprecedented' (NHK/CNA, Oct 10, 2026).
  • UN chief Guterres called the sanctions 'a serious blow'; Canada, France, Germany, Japan and the UK jointly said they 'strongly disagree' (CNA, Oct 10, 2026).
  • SDNY Judge Jesse M. Furman granted a preliminary injunction in a 30-page order (Oct 13) barring federal agencies from enforcing civil fines 'up to $377,700' or criminal penalties 'up to 20 years in prison' under IEEPA against DAWN and TAAG — finding the services ban 'constituted a content-based regulation of their speech-based activities and did not survive strict scrutiny' (JPost/Anadolu/Mail & Guardian/political.org — VERIFIED)
  • Furman rejected the request to declare EO 14203 facially unconstitutional; the injunction does not address the Oct 9 institutional designation (same — VERIFIED)
  • European Council summit declaration (Oct 16-17): EU leaders 'regretted US sanctions against the ICC, urging Washington to reverse the measures and reaffirming the EU's readiness to protect the court and ensure its continued access to financial and other services' (Daily Sabah/MENAFN — STRONGLY_SUPPORTED)
  • Athens: CONTESTED — Rapporteur (unnamed diplomat) says Rubio asked Greece to withdraw from the Rome Statute; FM Gerapetritis (Oct 17) says ICC 'was not discussed' (unresolved)
  • Navi Pillay (Oct 16): sanctions are 'unlawful' interference with an independent international court (The Citizen South Africa — ATTRIBUTED; single outlet)
  • Beth van Schaack (Oct 16, JusticeInfo): the 180-day grace period is a trap — 'very little to negotiate' (ANALYSIS/ATTRIBUTED)
  • OFAC General License 13 authorizes specified maintenance and wind-down transactions with the ICC through 12:01 a.m. EDT on April 7, 2027 (Chatham House, Oct 15; The Dispute Index — two independent analyses of the OFAC action)
  • License 14 separately permits specified telecommunications and enterprise-software transactions without that stated deadline; Licenses 15 and 16 address pension payments and certain detainees, with no stated expiry (Chatham House; The Dispute Index — two independent)
  • 17 legal organisations — the Law Society of Scotland, the Faculty of Advocates and 15 others — jointly condemned the US sanctions against the ICC, warning they risk undermining investigations, trials and victims' access to justice; called on the US administration to withdraw all sanctions and urged member states to use diplomatic means for reversal (scottishlegal.com, Oct 19 — on-record joint statement)
  • The European Commission said Monday it is considering measures, including activating its blocking statute, to protect the International Criminal Court from US sanctions; Commission spokesperson Siobhan McGarry told a Brussels midday briefing: 'The blocking statute still remains on the table', adding that preparatory work was underway but that no decision had been made on whether or when to activate it (TRT World / Anadolu Agency — VERIFIED as official Commission briefing)
  • The Commission is consulting member states and other ICC state parties on practical measures to protect the court's operations if the sanctions take effect, with the Dutch leading that work (same — VERIFIED as official briefing)
  • European Parliament Foreign Affairs and Development committees: International Criminal Court and its officials named among three finalists for the 2026 Sakharov Prize for Freedom of Thought (Oct 19, 2026; EP press release — VERIFIED primary source; corroborated by Dawn, Times of Israel)
  • Sakharov 2026 finalists: the ICC (S&D nomination), Tanzanian opposition leader Tundu Lissu (EPP/ECR), Iranian twins Taraneh and Romina Rahimi (Patriots for Europe); laureate chosen Oct 22 by the Conference of Presidents; ceremony Dec 16, Strasbourg (EP press release; Dawn — VERIFIED)
  • MEP Barry Andrews (Renew, Ireland): shortlisting the ICC is support for 'a fraying rules-based international order' in 'an increasingly fraught global environment' (Dawn, Oct 20 — VERIFIED, on-record)
  • European Parliament Foreign Affairs committee: adopted 50-18 (4 abstentions) a report on protecting international justice calling for activation of the EU blocking statute and continued banking/insurance/digital access for ICC staff (Agence Europe, Oct 20 — VERIFIED; committee report, not plenary legislation)
  • POLITICO (via Mezha, Oct 23): ICC registrar Osvaldo Zavala Giler pressed the European Commission to issue clear guidance 'as soon as possible' on US sanctions against the court, ahead of EU foreign-policy chief Kaja Kallas's visit to ICC headquarters; he said the Commission's 'lack of clarity' leaves companies and NGOs unsure whether to comply. The court spent six months moving away from US technology providers toward open-source software and 20 months making transactions independent of the dollar; a telecom exemption exists but could be withdrawn. STATUS: VERIFIED as reported statements.
  • Global Legal Post (Oct 23): the International Bar Association denounced the US sanctions — executive director Mark Ellis said they 'undo decades of progress for victims and weaken the rule of law globally', calling the ICC 'the cornerstone of the international justice architecture' and calling on states to oppose the sanctions. STATUS: VERIFIED as the IBA's statement.

What remains disputed

The targets, the legal basis, allied reaction, and whether this is tied to the Iran war, the Gaza file, or a broader institutional confrontation — all unestablished.

Why it matters

Sanctioning the court that investigates war crimes — while the US is itself a party to a war — is a statement about the rules-based order as much as about the ICC. Allies who back the court will be forced to choose.

Strategic context

US–ICC friction is not new; it spans administrations. What is new is the context: an active US war, an ICC with live files on the region, and an administration comfortable using sanctions as a multi-front tool.

Competing interpretations

Reading one: defensive — preempting ICC scrutiny of the Iran war. Reading two: ideological — a broader assault on international institutions constraining US action. Reading three: leverage — sanctions as a bargaining chip for something else entirely.

SATURDAY — DEPUTY PROSECUTOR SAYS COURT’S WORK CONTINUES DESPITE SANCTIONS; STAFF ALREADY FEELING BANKING SQUEEZE — ICC Deputy Prosecutor Nazhat Shameem Khan, co-running the prosecutor’s office pending the appointment of a new chief prosecutor, said in an interview with the Guardian that the Gaza war investigation and others have made “significant progress” since the war-crimes arrest warrants of November 2024, with further applications kept secret by court order (The Guardian, carried by VidiaNews, Oct 24 — VERIFIED as her on-record statement; the progress itself is her claim, not independently established). She said some staff are already experiencing banking difficulties because banks are “over complying” while the scope of the measures remains uncertain — even though the institutional sanctions are not due to take full effect until six months from Oct 9, the date of their announcement. She said no decision has been made to reduce capability or output: “All the investigations across all situations continue.” The court has switched its software from Microsoft to European alternatives and is negotiating with service providers over banking and health insurance, she said. CONTEXT: Khan herself was among the 13 ICC staff sanctioned in February 2025 (same interview). Sits under v12: the sanctions fight moves from institutional pushback to the court’s day-to-day insulation — work continuing, friction arriving early through bank caution rather than through the sanctions’ formal start date. The ICC fights the sanctions on two fronts — registrar Osvaldo Zavala Giler demanded urgent EU guidance for companies/NGOs caught between the sanctions and EU protections (POLITICO, Oct 23), while the International Bar Association publicly denounced the sanctions as undoing 'decades of progress' (Global Legal Post, Oct 23). Both VERIFIED as on-record statements. ICC WINS 2026 SAKHAROV PRIZE — the International Criminal Court and its officials won the EU's 2026 Sakharov Prize for Freedom of Thought, European Parliament President Roberta Metsola announced Thursday to a standing ovation in Strasbourg; the nomination came from the Socialists and Democrats group, and the award ceremony is set for December 16 in Strasbourg (Reuters, Oct 22 — VERIFIED, primary-source announcement plus wire corroboration). The second international recognition for the court this month after ex-ICC judge Navi Pillay's Nobel Peace Prize, and it lands weeks after the October 9 US sanctions that banned transactions with the court after a six-month grace period; ICC President Tomoko Akane said the "unprecedented challenges" the court faces made the prize more significant (Reuters — VERIFIED as her statement). Sits under v10: the shortlist became the prize — Europe's institutional answer to the sanctions track. ICC SHORTLISTED FOR 2026 SAKHAROV PRIZE — the European Parliament's Foreign Affairs and Development committees named the International Criminal Court and its officials one of three finalists for the 2026 Sakharov Prize for Freedom of Thought on Monday evening, Oct 19 (European Parliament press release — VERIFIED, primary source; corroborated by Dawn, Times of Israel, Agence Europe). The other finalists: Tanzanian opposition leader Tundu Lissu (EPP/ECR-backed) and Iranian twin sisters Taraneh and Romina Rahimi, imprisoned in Iran (Patriots for Europe-backed). The ICC candidacy was put forward by the parliament's Socialists and Democrats (S&D) group; centrist MEP Barry Andrews framed the shortlisting as support 'for a fraying rules-based international order' (Dawn, Oct 20 — VERIFIED). The final laureate is chosen Thursday, Oct 22, by the Conference of Presidents, with the ceremony Dec 16 in Strasbourg. BLOCKING-STATUTE PUSH — the day before, MEPs on the Foreign Affairs committee adopted (50-18, 4 abstentions) a report on protecting international justice calling for the EU's blocking statute to be activated and for European banks, insurers and digital providers to keep serving sanctioned court staff and organisations (Agence Europe, Oct 20 — VERIFIED; committee vote, not plenary law). Human-rights subcommittee chair Mounir Satouri (Greens/EFA) criticized the Commission's Kaja Kallas for inaction (same — ATTRIBUTED). EU EYES BLOCKING STATUTE — the European Commission said Monday it is considering measures, including activating its blocking statute, to protect the International Criminal Court from US sanctions; Commission spokesperson Siobhan McGarry told a Brussels midday briefing that 'the blocking statute still remains on the table', with preparatory work underway but no decision made on whether or when to activate it; the Commission is consulting member states and ICC state parties on practical measures, with the Dutch leading (TRT World / Anadolu — VERIFIED as official Commission briefing). LEGAL BODIES CONDEMN SANCTIONS — the Law Society of Scotland, the Faculty of Advocates and 15 other legal organisations jointly condemned the US sanctions against the International Criminal Court, warning they risk undermining investigations, trials and victims' access to justice; the signatories called on the US administration to withdraw all sanctions and urged member states to use diplomatic means to secure their reversal (scottishlegal.com, Oct 19 — on-record joint statement). EU WEIGHS BLOCKING STATUTE TO SHIELD ICC — the European Commission said Monday it is considering measures, including activating its blocking statute, to protect the International Criminal Court from the US sanctions: Commission spokesperson Siobhan McGarry told the midday Brussels briefing the EU has been in contact with member states over potential activation following Washington's announcement (Anadolu Agency, Brussels dateline, Oct 19 — VERIFIED as her on-record briefing statement; single-carrier at build time). McGarry said preparatory work is underway but stressed no decision has been made: 'The blocking statute still remains on the table'; 'We have no definitive date or time for the activation, or we won't prejudge whether we will activate it. We're obviously still hoping that we can find a more optimal outcome to allow the court to continue its vital work' (same — VERIFIED as statement). The statute is designed to protect EU companies and individuals from complying with certain foreign sanctions; McGarry said the Commission is also consulting several ICC state parties on practical measures to protect court operations, with the Netherlands 'very, very much leading on this work' (same). First institutional countermove to the sanctions since Rubio's Oct 9 announcement (v1-v6). Builds on v3's US court injunction. LICENSE MECHANICS CLARIFIED — OFAC's designation comes with General License 13, which authorizes specified maintenance and wind-down transactions through 12:01 a.m. EDT on April 7, 2027; License 14 separately permits specified telecommunications and enterprise-software transactions without that stated deadline; Licenses 15 and 16 address pension payments and certain detainees, with no stated expiry (Chatham House, Oct 15; The Dispute Index — two independent analyses of the OFAC action; the 180-day/april-2027 wind-down window corroborates the 'six-month timeline' in v1). The licenses limit the immediate bite of the institutional designation while leaving the earlier individual designations — judges, prosecutor — in place (same sources). Cross-linked to the Furman injunction (v5), which blocks enforcement against two rights groups. JUDGE FURMAN BLOCKS ENFORCEMENT AGAINST TWO RIGHTS GROUPS — SDNY Judge Jesse M. Furman 'granted a preliminary injunction in a 30-page order' (Oct 13; reported Oct 14-15), finding the groups likely to succeed on First Amendment claims; the ban on providing services to sanctioned ICC-related persons 'constituted a content-based regulation of their speech-based activities and did not survive strict scrutiny.' Bars federal agencies from enforcing civil fines 'up to $377,700' or criminal penalties 'up to 20 years in prison' under IEEPA against plaintiffs DAWN and TAAG, which announced they will resume submitting war-crimes evidence to the court (JPost/Anadolu/Mail & Guardian/political.org — VERIFIED). Furman 'rejected the plaintiffs' request to declare the 2025 executive order as facially unconstitutional' — EO 14203 remains in place; the injunction does not address the Oct 9 institutional designation. The first judicial wound in the sanctions campaign since Rubio's Oct 9 institutional designation — a narrow, plaintiff-specific crack, not a demolition. EU COUNCIL SUMMIT DECLARATION (OCT 16-17) — EU leaders 'regretted US sanctions against the International Criminal Court (ICC), urging Washington to reverse the measures and reaffirming the EU's readiness to protect the court and ensure its continued access to financial and other services' (Daily Sabah, Oct 16; MENAFN, Oct 17 — STRONGLY_SUPPORTED; no primary consilium text fetched). The first leader-level, institution-wide countermeasure pledge — aimed squarely at neutralizing the sanctions' stated purpose ('crippling its ability to operate'); pairs with Dutch PM Jetten's 'we will do whatever we need to defend the ICC'. ATHENS: CONTESTED — Rapporteur (~Oct 14, exclusive): 'Rubio sought Greece's support in the Trump administration's campaign against the ICC during his recent visit to Athens... Rubio would like Greece to withdraw from the Rome Statute' — one unnamed diplomat; vs. Gerapetritis (Oct 17): ICC 'was not discussed during his talks in Athens with Marco Rubio'; 'Athens Backs ICC Independence, Urges US to Reconsider Sanctions' (tovima/euagenda — CONTESTED on whether Rubio raised it). The two stories cannot both be true — Gerapetritis's public denial, issued three days after the leak, favors the latter interpretation but does not settle it. What is stable: Athens publicly aligned with the EU consensus line. PILLAY HITS BACK (OCT 16) — 'He [Trump] should call in his advisors to tell him why we say that it is unlawful for him to interfere with an independent international court.' / 'You can't selectively pursue one lot of people and not the other. It will be such a bad, dangerous precedent.' (The Citizen South Africa, opinion format — ATTRIBUTED; single outlet, needs a second before established). VAN SCHAACK: GRACE PERIOD IS A TRAP (OCT 16) — former US Ambassador-at-Large for Global Criminal Justice Beth van Schaack to JusticeInfo: 'States [parties to the ICC] may attempt to make certain concessions to this administration, sacrificing their values or dignity in the process, only to find in the end that this administration will not budge an inch.' / 'There is very little to negotiate... if anything, it may throw them under a bus further.' (ANALYSIS/ATTRIBUTED). PILLAY: "ILLEGAL" SANCTIONS — Nobel laureate Navi Pillay told AFP on Oct 14 that Trump's sanctions against the ICC are "illegal," that the president needed a lesson in international law ("Call in your legal advisors to explain to you what international law is"), and that the sanctions' timing — announced hours after her Nobel — was a "reaction" to the award not going to Trump; she said prior US administrations welcomed an ICC arrest warrant against Putin and called the current posture selective double standards (Times of Israel / IOL via AFP, Oct 14-15 — VERIFIED as on-record statements). OCT 20 DEADLINE — the parties in the Furman injunction litigation have until Oct 20 to address the next procedural steps, including a possible permanent injunction (Mail & Guardian, Oct 15 — reported); the injunction remains temporary and protects only the plaintiffs. WIND-DOWN — a legal analysis notes EO 14203's asset freezes and transaction bans carry limited exceptions: the ongoing detention of some awaiting trial, pension payments, IT and social-media use, and a 180-day "wind-down" permitting certain otherwise-prohibited transactions until April 2027 (almendron, Oct 16 — attributed analysis). FEDERAL JUDGE BLOCKS ICC SANCTIONS AS APPLIED TO TWO US RIGHTS GROUPS — US District Judge Jesse Furman issued a temporary injunction blocking the Trump administration's ICC sanctions as applied to two American rights organizations — DAWN and TAAG — that aid the ICC's Gaza war-crimes inquiry (political.org, Oct 14; countercurrents.org, Oct 15). The challenge targets Executive Order 14203 (signed Feb 6, 2025); the order itself remains in place. Furman ruled that, as applied to the plaintiffs, the ban on providing 'services' to designated persons regulates speech based on content and cannot satisfy strict scrutiny (First Amendment); the groups also invoked the Fifth Amendment's due process clause. DAWN said it regularly provides the ICC evidence of alleged war crimes under Article 15 of the Rome Statute; TAAG had suspended its planned cooperation out of fear of prosecution (same). The injunction is temporary and protects only the plaintiffs while the suit proceeds — it does not strike down the broader sanctions framework (same). Two outlets carrying; keep as reported pending wire confirmation. THE SANCTIONS ARE REAL — the US on Oct 9 sanctioned the ICC as an institution (the entire tribunal, for the first time), adding it to the Treasury's list of sanctioned entities and moving to ban transactions with the court, with a six-month wind-down/grace period for businesses dealing with it (AP via Military.com, Oct 9; CNA, Oct 10; Le Monde, Oct 10). Rubio's demands: cancel the Nov 21, 2024 arrest warrants against Netanyahu and Gallant; close the investigation into US military actions in Afghanistan and CIA secret prisons; reform the Rome Statute to bar prosecution of nationals of non-party states (Le Monde, Oct 10). Full effect by April 9, 2027 unless the court complies — Rubio: 'Either the ICC will end its threats, or we will end the ICC' (Le Monde). The announcement came hours after the Nobel Peace Prize went to former ICC judge Navanethem 'Navi' Pillay — the move long predated the prize, AP notes (CNA; USA Today). Pushback: the ICC rejected the sanctions, warning 'history will judge this moment'; President Akane Tomoko called them 'historically unprecedented' (NHK); UN chief Guterres called them 'a serious blow to our collective pursuit of international criminal justice' (CNA); Canada, France, Germany, Japan and the UK said in a joint statement they 'strongly disagree' (CNA). RUBIO PRESSES THE ICC LINE WITH JAPAN — US Secretary of State Marco Rubio raised what a State Department readout called 'the necessity of U.S. sanctions on the International Criminal Court' in a weekend phone call with Japanese Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi, describing the sanctions as needed because of the Court's 'rogue actions' threatening American sovereignty (Japan Times, Oct 11). A Japanese Foreign Ministry readout released late Saturday said Motegi 'conveyed the Japanese government's position on the recent U.S. institutional sanctions,' with the two agreeing 'to maintain close communication' — a softer line that keeps Tokyo inside the eight-country joint statement rejecting the sanctions (v5). Two readouts, two framings; no new US policy move — Washington's sanctions stand, with the 180-day licensing grace period (v5) leaving the practical fight for the coming weeks. monitor opened.

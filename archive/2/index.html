Gaza ceasefire diplomacy at one year — Kushner touts progress, warns 'hard work ahead' on disarmament

One year after the October 9, 2025 agreement, Kushner marked the anniversary with a list of milestones — and conceded the hardest part, Hamas disarmament, is still unfinished.

Updated 11 Oct 2026, 15:20 UTC Analysis 15 sources
Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/gaza-ceasefire-diplomacy/

What happened

October 9, 2026 marked one year since the October 9, 2025 agreement on Trump's 20-point Gaza peace plan. Jared Kushner, in an anniversary post, said 'significant progress' had been made and listed milestones: the October 9, 2025 agreement launching a ceasefire that largely endured; the October 12, 2025 Israeli withdrawal to the 'yellow line'; the return of all 20 living Israeli hostages on October 13, 2025; UN Security Council Resolution 2803 endorsing the plan (Nov 17, 2025); the return of the final deceased hostage's remains in January 2026; the establishment of the Board of Peace, an international stabilization force, and a technocratic Palestinian administration for Gaza. Kushner said Hamas had signed a phased disarmament framework in July 2026 — but acknowledged 'There is still hard work ahead,' saying disarmament 'must be implemented, security established, essential services restored, and communities rebuilt.' Separately, Dawn reported the disarmament track is stalled: Hamas agreed in July to hand weapons to the technocratic government, but Israel objects to the Board of Peace text calling for phased withdrawal and demands 'full demilitarisation' before pulling back; Kushner met Hamas and Israeli leaders in August without breakthrough, both sides holding to their demands. (All milestone claims are Kushner's; the stall assessment is Dawn's.) On Saturday Israeli strikes killed at least five Palestinians in Gaza — four near Panda Mall in Gaza City and one in the Tuffah neighbourhood, per health officials (Dawn, Oct 11). Israel claims Hamas has been rearming and launching attacks in violation of the October ceasefire; Hamas denies it and accuses Israel of undermining the deal. The running tallies: at least 1,400 Palestinians killed in Gaza since the ceasefire per the territory's health officials (Israel says four of its soldiers have been killed); Palestinian authorities say more than 74,000 have been killed since October 2023 (Dawn). Al Jazeera's Oct 10 stock-take of Trump's Board of Peace: the board presented a $2.45 billion recovery plan in September, but reconstruction is stalled; no stabilisation force has deployed — six countries (Indonesia, Morocco, Kazakhstan, Albania, Kosovo and Uganda) have committed personnel toward a 20,000-soldier + 12,000-police force to be headed by CENTCOM; Israel now occupies about 60% of the Strip with the 'Yellow Line' pushed farther in, and Palestinians crossing it unknowingly have been shot dead (Al Jazeera).

What remains disputed

Nearly everything material: who proposed what, whether the gaps are closing, and what 'hard work ahead' refers to in concrete terms. No primary documents or on-record statements beyond the reported Kushner remark have been established here.

Why it matters

Gaza diplomacy and the Iran war are now linked pressure systems. A Gaza ceasefire would change the regional temperature — and the Houthi/Saudi/Yemen escalations show the cost of failure.

Strategic context

Months of on-and-off Gaza negotiations have repeatedly stalled at the same obstacles: hostages, withdrawal terms, and post-war governance. The current round is being watched for whether regional pressure from the Iran war finally forces movement.

Competing interpretations

Reading one: real momentum, with the Iran war creating incentives for a deal. Reading two: managed optics — talks as process, not progress. The evidence is not yet sufficient to choose.

Trump says US could end Iran war now — but only with guarantees Tehran never goes nuclear

At Joint Base Andrews, Trump said the seven-month war could end immediately with a no-nuclear-weapons guarantee; he claimed 18 US personnel killed and 159 Iranian ships sunk — figures not independently confirmed — and floated renaming the Strait of Hormuz 'the Trump Strait.'

Updated 11 Oct 2026, 14:05 UTC Verified 16 sources
Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/us-israel-iran-war-october/

What happened

Strikes against Iran by the US and Israel began on February 28, 2026, and the conflict continues into October. Al Jazeera's liveblog (Oct 9, 2026) tracks ongoing developments, including Iranian media reports of massive explosions in the Strait of Hormuz. Separately, Trump has said he ruled out an attack on Iran before the November 3 midterm elections, citing 'productive discussions' with Tehran. On October 10, after a second missile attack on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport, US President Trump told reporters the United States 'may' join Saudi Arabia in striking the Houthis. 'We may. We're going to look at it. We just found out about the recent attack. We will make a decision. We move very quickly,' he said outside the White House. Trump also said he would consider restarting US strikes on Iran — despite his earlier public pledge ruling out an attack on Iran before the November 3 midterm elections (Al Jazeera liveblog, New York Post, Times Now, A News, Mathrubhumi, Times of Israel, Oct 10). On October 11, Trump set a public end-condition at Joint Base Andrews after a campaign rally in Tennessee: the US 'could leave right now,' he said, 'but we have to guarantee that they can't have it [a nuclear weapon] in the future. And we'll get that.' He claimed 18 American personnel have been killed in the nearly seven-month conflict, said he had met families of the fallen at Dover Air Force Base, and claimed US operations had sunk 159 Iranian ships since late February and destroyed much of Iran's navy, air force, radar systems and manufacturing infrastructure. He also said Tehran would 'probably do a little bit extra before the election, thinking it's going to hurt us,' and floated renaming the Strait of Hormuz 'the Trump Strait' (IANS, lokmattimes, Oct 11). The casualty count, ship-sinking figure and capability claims are Trump's own assertions and have not been independently confirmed.

Why it matters

A war in its eighth month now has a political calendar attached: the US midterms. Trump's stated pause — real or rhetorical — creates a window in which diplomacy (Putin mediation, Gaza ceasefire threads, diesel deals) is suddenly load-bearing. It also sets up a cliff edge: what happens after November 3.

Strategic context

The war began Feb 28, 2026 and has widened across the region: Saudi-led operations in Yemen, Houthi-linked strikes reaching Riyadh, IRGC action in the Strait of Hormuz. Energy markets, shipping insurance, and regional alliances are all downstream of this conflict.

Competing interpretations

Reading one: the pre-midterm pause is genuine — Washington wants de-escalation and is working channels. Reading two: it is electoral management — no new wars before November 3, then reassess. Reading three: Tehran reads the pause as a sign of exhaustion and will use the window to rebuild (see the missile-supply reporting), making the post-midterm cliff sharper.

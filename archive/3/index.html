IRGC Navy says the Strait of Hormuz is closed again as tanker attacks resume

The strait is shut again after Friday's brief reopening, and will stay that way until the US lifts its blockade of Iranian ports — while a tanker struck by a projectile Saturday night was the first reported attack in more than ten days.

Updated 11 Oct 2026, 11:55 UTC Analysis 15 sources
Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/hormuz-shipping-insurance/

What happened

This is a developing-story monitor. The thread: Iranian media reports of massive explosions in the Strait of Hormuz (cited in Al Jazeera's Oct 9 liveblog) and the reported IRGC strike on the LPG carrier NV Sunshine are the kind of events that move war-risk insurance pricing and tanker routing. Awaiting verification: actual insurance rate moves, rerouting data, and confirmed damage to shipping. Incident volume is now quantified: at least 13 incidents involving oil, LNG and LPG tankers were recorded around the strait between Sept 28 and Oct 5, according to data analyzed by three maritime security sources and cited by Reuters — the highest weekly total since the war began on Feb 28. The UN's International Maritime Organization recorded nine incidents in the same week. The US Navy-led Joint Maritime Information Center said the activity continued into the weekend, demonstrating 'Iran's intent to assert presence along key transit lanes.' Iranian state media reported (Times Now, Oct 10, 23:13 IST) that the IRGC said an unidentified oil tanker exploded and caught fire after hitting a naval mine in the strait. The NV Sunshine strike claim is now contested by the vessel's owner (see st-004). War-risk insurance premium data and confirmed rerouting figures are still not established in this newsroom's sourcing. CENTCOM said Sunday the disabled Ocean Molica/Arika Sun ignored repeated warnings before the stern strike and released footage of the explosion (The National). UKMTO issued a Sunday advisory on an incident involving a tanker and military forces in the Gulf of Oman, details unconfirmed. The IRGC claimed via Sepah News that a 'violating' supertanker exploded after hitting naval mines in the strait — alleged, no independent confirmation. Reuters reports a Tehran–Washington process with proposals to reopen the strait 'within seven days' (via JPost).

What is verified

  • Iranian media reported massive explosions in the Hormuz strait (cited in Al Jazeera liveblog, Oct 9, 2026).
  • The IRGC reportedly struck the LPG carrier NV Sunshine near the strait (Al Jazeera, Oct 9, 2026).
  • No verified insurance-pricing or rerouting data is established in this newsroom's sourcing yet.
  • At least 13 tanker incidents (attacks, attempted attacks, drone overflights, VHF hails) around the strait Sept 28–Oct 5 — highest weekly total since Feb 28 — per maritime security data cited by Reuters (Times Now, Oct 10, 2026).
  • IMO recorded nine vessel incidents in the same week; the JMIC says such activity continued into the weekend (Times Now, Oct 10).
  • NV Sunshine strike claim contested by vessel owner NVTrans (AAP, Oct 10) — see st-004.
  • Strait of Hormuz transits fell to the lowest in more than two months after tanker attacks hit their highest weekly level since Feb 28 (Reuters, Oct 11, 2026).
  • CENTCOM blockade enforcement data: resumed July 14; four commercial vessels disabled, 135 ships turned around, 10 IRGC-linked tankers destroyed over three months (Al Jazeera, Oct 11, 2026).
  • UKMTO: several shipmasters near Ras Al Khaimah (UAE) reported VHF radio broadcasts instructing vessels to leave their anchorages (Times Now, Oct 11, 2026).
  • CENTCOM's Sunday statement said the Ocean Molica/Arika Sun ignored repeated warnings before the precision stern strike and released video of the explosion (The National, Oct 11).
  • UKMTO issued a Sunday advisory about an incident involving a tanker and military forces in the Gulf of Oman; details and timing unconfirmed (The National, Oct 11).
  • Reuters reports a Tehran–Washington diplomatic process with proposals under which the Strait of Hormuz could be reopened 'within seven days' (via JPost, Oct 11).
  • Kpler data: just seven commodity-carrying vessels transited the strait on Oct 6, the lowest daily figure since July 23 (Reuters via Sunday Guardian, Oct 11).
  • Tehran will not reopen the waterway until the US meets its demands and takes steps to restore Iran's trust (NDTV, Oct 11).
  • A tanker was hit off Qatar, about 500km west of the strait, days before Friday's incidents (NDTV).
  • The IRGC navy says the Strait of Hormuz is closed again after its brief Friday reopening and will stay closed until the US lifts its blockade of Iranian ports (live coverage, Oct 11).
  • UKMTO reported an outbound tanker was struck by an unknown projectile in the strait at 11:20pm UAE time on Saturday, with a fire on board and all crew safe (The National, Oct 11).
  • India's foreign ministry says two Indian-flagged ships were involved in a 'shooting incident' in the strait (live coverage, Oct 11).

  • The Indian consulate confirmed all crew members rescued and safe (X post, Oct 11, 2026).
  • UKMTO said the GEM NO. 2 fire was extinguished; no group has claimed responsibility and the cause (IRGC mine claim vs projectile/drone strike) remains disputed.

What remains disputed

Whether insurers have materially repriced Hormuz risk, whether tankers are rerouting, and the scale of any disruption — all await data. The explosions themselves are reported by Iranian media, not independently confirmed. The naval-mine explosion report comes solely from Iranian state media via the IRGC; the vessel is unidentified and no independent confirmation exists. Whether insurers have materially repriced Hormuz risk remains unestablished — incident counts are not insurance data. A new Oct 11 IRGC claim of striking the LPG carrier NV Sunshine directly conflicts with owner NVTrans's Oct 10 denial that any attack occurred — treat as alleged. The IRGC's naval-mine claim is alleged — only Iranian state media (Sepah News, with Xinhua/IANS carries) reports it; the vessel is unnamed and unidentified, and no independent confirmation exists. Its warning that enforcement will expand beyond Hormuz is escalatory messaging from the same unverified statement, not evidence of capability or intent beyond the words themselves.

Why it matters

Roughly a fifth of global petroleum liquids moves through Hormuz. Even the perception of risk — let alone confirmed strikes on commercial vessels — transmits into oil prices, diesel markets, and the inflation the diesel deal is trying to tame.

Strategic context

The strait has been the Iran war's maritime pressure point since February. Commercial strikes are the sharpest escalation of that pressure: they turn a war between states into a tax on the global economy.

Competing interpretations

Reading one: markets will price this as a contained risk — until a second strike lands. Reading two: insurers move faster than states; a quiet repricing is already underway. Data will decide.

Treasury license confirmed: Russian diesel authorized through April 7, 2027

The license text is public: Russian-origin diesel transactions are authorized through 12:01am EDT April 7, 2027 — the year-plus carve-out is real.

Updated 11 Oct 2026, 10:25 UTC Verified 5 sources
Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/treasury-license-russian-diesel/

What happened

The Treasury license is confirmed. The US Department of Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control issued a license authorizing, through 12:01 am eastern daylight time on April 7, 2027, 'all transactions prohibited by the Russian Harmful Foreign Activities Sanctions Regulations … that are related to the sale, delivery, offloading, or importation, including importation into the United States, of diesel fuel of Russian Federation origin' (The Hindu, quoting the Treasury notice, Oct 10). The license was issued minutes after Trump's call with Putin on Oct 9 (The Hindu). The National adds the license does not authorize any debit to an account on the books of a US financial institution of Russia's central bank, its national wealth fund, or its finance ministry. The carve-out therefore runs through April 7, 2027 — well past the Nov 3 US midterms — while keeping the core financial sanctions architecture in place.

What is verified

  • OFAC license text published: Russian-origin diesel transactions authorized through 12:01 am EDT April 7, 2027 (The Hindu, USA Today, The National, Firstpost).
  • License issued Oct 9, minutes after the Trump–Putin call (The Hindu).
  • Russian central bank, national wealth fund, and finance ministry accounts remain blocked (The National).
  • Zelenskyy objected: 'an investment in war that needs to end, not continue' (The National, Firstpost).
  • OFAC instrument identified as General License 135, issued Oct 9, 2026 (Axios via Shafaq News).

What remains disputed

Whether the license covers diesel only or wider refined products — the text as reported covers 'diesel fuel of Russian Federation origin.' Renewal terms and whether Congress or European allies were consulted are unestablished. The expiry date, however, is no longer in dispute.

Why it matters

Duration is the story. A short waiver is crisis management; a license through April 2027 is a de facto sanctions regime change for Russian refined products. It would give Moscow a year-plus revenue channel and give US fuel markets a year-plus of Russian supply — with everything that implies for Ukraine policy and European coordination.

Strategic context

US sanctions on Russian energy have been the West's central economic weapon since 2022. A long-dated license punches a hole in that regime — and hands Moscow leverage: every renewal date becomes a negotiation.

Competing interpretations

Reading one: pragmatic duration — markets need certainty, and a long license prevents a price cliff. Reading two: this is normalization by the back door, now confirmed as a year-plus regime — executed without a public policy debate. Reading three: the date is a ceiling, not a commitment — licenses can be revoked, and April 7, 2027 is an election-cycle away.

What changed since last update

v3 (Oct 11, 14:25): OFAC instrument identified as General License 135, issued October 9 (Axios via Shafaq News). No other terms updated. v2 (Oct 11): license CONFIRMED via published Treasury/OFAC text. Transactions related to sale, delivery, offloading, or importation of Russian-origin diesel are authorized through 12:01 am EDT April 7, 2027 (The Hindu, USA Today, The National, Firstpost, Oct 9–10); Russian central bank, national wealth fund, and finance ministry accounts remain blocked.

What to watch next

  • European reaction — does Brussels follow the US carve-out or object?
  • Ukrainian government follow-up beyond Zelenskyy's condemnation.
  • Whether the license covers only diesel or wider refined products in practice.
  • First actual cargoes: tanker tracking for Russian diesel under the license.

Russia is supplying missiles to Iran as Tehran rebuilds its arsenal, report says

Iran retains production capacity and substantial ballistic missile stocks despite eight months of US-Israeli airstrikes, according to Bloomberg reporting cited by Haaretz.

Updated 10 Oct 2026, 23:18 UTC Verified 5 sources
Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/russia-iran-missiles-rebuild/

What happened

Russia is supplying Iran with missiles as Tehran rebuilds its arsenal, according to Bloomberg reporting cited by Haaretz on October 10, 2026. The reporting states Iran retains production capacity and substantial ballistic missile stocks despite eight months of US-Israeli airstrikes. The White House has rejected the assessment on the record: spokesperson Anna Kelly said via Bloomberg (Oct 10) that US operations destroyed Iran's ballistic missiles, production facilities and navy (carried by Newsmax, The Times of Israel, Ynet, The Jerusalem Post). Separately, Bloomberg reports — citing the British navy — that at least 10 vessels were struck near the Strait of Hormuz in the past week, a figure carried by Haaretz and Newsmax.

What is verified

  • Bloomberg reporting, cited by Haaretz (Oct 10, 2026), says Russia is supplying missiles to Iran.
  • The reporting states Iran retains ballistic missile production capacity and substantial stocks.
  • US-Israeli airstrikes against Iran have been ongoing for roughly eight months (since Feb 28, 2026).
  • White House spokesperson Anna Kelly rejected the assessment on the record via Bloomberg: she said US operations destroyed Iran's ballistic missiles, production facilities and navy (carried by Newsmax, The Times of Israel, Ynet, The Jerusalem Post, Oct 10).
  • Bloomberg reports, citing the British navy, that at least 10 vessels were struck near the Strait of Hormuz in the past week (carried by Haaretz, Newsmax, Oct 10).

What remains disputed

The types, quantities, and delivery timeline of the missiles have not been publicly specified. The claim that eight months of strikes failed to degrade Iran's missile capability is now directly contested by the White House — spokesperson Anna Kelly said via Bloomberg on Oct 10 that US operations destroyed Iran's ballistic missiles, production facilities and navy. No official US/Israeli assessment with battle-damage evidence is reflected in the cited reporting; the dispute is on-the-record statements vs. intelligence assessments, not settled by public evidence. Russia's strategic intent — defensive resupply vs. deepening war partnership — is unstated.

Why it matters

If accurate, this is the clearest evidence yet that the US-Israeli air campaign has not achieved its stated aim of degrading Iran's strike capability — and that Moscow is willing to backfill it. That combination hardens Iran's negotiating position and lengthens the war's expected runway.

Strategic context

Russia and Iran deepened military cooperation after 2022, most visibly through Iranian drones in Ukraine. The reverse flow — Russian munitions into Iran — marks a new phase. It follows the Putin–Pezeshkian meeting in Ashgabat, where Moscow pledged mediation while visibly tightening its embrace of Tehran.

Competing interpretations

Reading one: this is routine alliance maintenance — Moscow resupplying a partner to keep the war grinding and keep Washington distracted. Reading two: it is a deterrent signal to Washington and Jerusalem ahead of any escalation. Reading three (skeptical): the reporting may overstate deliveries or conflate agreements with delivered hardware; the physical evidence is not public.

What changed since last update

What to watch next

  • Any US Treasury or State Department response — fresh sanctions designations would confirm the assessment.
  • Satellite or shipping evidence of missile transfers.
  • Whether the White House's public rebuttal is followed by any new declassified strike damage assessments.
  • Iranian missile activity in the ongoing war: does tempo or targeting change?
  • Whether Moscow's mediation offer at Ashgabat is real leverage or cover for arming.

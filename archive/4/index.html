The US-Israel war on Iran: strikes began February 28 — Trump rules out escalation before the midterms

Al Jazeera's live reporting continues to track the war; Trump says he has ruled out a fresh attack before the November 3 midterms while citing 'productive discussions' with Tehran.

Updated 14 Oct 2026, 06:54 UTC Verified 19 sources
Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/us-israel-iran-war-october/

What happened

Strikes against Iran by the US and Israel began on February 28, 2026, and the conflict continues into October. Al Jazeera's liveblog (Oct 9, 2026) tracks ongoing developments, including Iranian media reports of massive explosions in the Strait of Hormuz. Separately, Trump has said he ruled out an attack on Iran before the November 3 midterm elections, citing 'productive discussions' with Tehran.

What is verified

  • US-Israeli strikes on Iran began February 28, 2026.
  • Al Jazeera liveblog (Oct 9, 2026) tracks the ongoing war, including Iranian media reports of massive explosions in the Hormuz strait.
  • Trump has publicly ruled out an attack on Iran before the Nov 3 midterms, citing 'productive discussions'.
  • Iran is still reviewing the US response to its proposal on reopening the Strait of Hormuz and will reply to Washington via intermediaries (FM spokesman Baghaei, Reuters, Oct 12, 2026).
  • Message exchange between Iran and the US through mediators is continuing; Qatar and Pakistan among mediators (same).
  • Tehran's seven-point plan unchanged: lift the naval blockade, end the war on all fronts, release frozen Iranian assets (Al Jazeera/Dawn, Oct 12, 2026).
  • Central disagreement: Washington insists on addressing Iran's nuclear programme from the outset; Tehran says talks should focus initially on reopening Hormuz (Reuters, Oct 12, 2026).
  • Iranian FM Abbas Araghchi spoke by phone with Iraqi FM Fuad Hussein on 'the latest regional developments,' including the worsening situation in Yemen (Fuad Hussein Telegram via Al Jazeera liveblog, Oct 12, 2026).
  • Trump said Monday the Nov 3 midterms will have 'no impact' on his Iran approach; the war will end 'very soon' and oil prices will then 'drop like a rock' (Times of Israel liveblog; Indian Express, Oct 12, 2026).
  • Trump said he spoke with Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman about the Houthi strikes: 'The Houthis are misbehaving very badly... No good.' (same).
  • UK PM Andy Burnham told Parliament Monday the RAF Fairford suspected plot was 'highly likely to have been co-ordinated in Iran, almost certainly directed' by the IRGC (AP via TNND; Rudaw; Press Association-syndicated reports, Oct 12, 2026).
  • The Sept 27 Fairford incident involved three suspicious vans; police found gasoline but no explosives; seven men in their 20s arrested, all on bail, none charged — six British nationals and one dual British-Iranian citizen (same).
  • The UK summoned Iran's ambassador and sanctioned two Iranians — IRGC counterespionage chief Ruhollah Bazghandi and Zindashti-network facilitator Shahram Ali Reza Tamarzadeh Jakki — with asset freezes and travel bans (same; Clacton Gazette, Oct 12, 2026).
  • A US State Department joint statement condemned Iran for 'lethal plotting, arson, sabotage, cyber-attacks,' endorsed by more than 20 countries (29 per Indian Express and JFeed, Oct 12, 2026).
  • Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood told MPs a 'known individual based in Iran' was thought to have co-ordinated the plot; Iran denied involvement and asked the UK for evidence (same).
  • The Pentagon confirmed US B-52/B-1 bombers were pulled from RAF Fairford last week and redeployed to home bases (via Reflector/AP, Oct 12, 2026).
  • Trump said late Monday he does not believe China and Russia are helping Iran 'at all,' writing on Truth Social they have 'too much to lose' and that the US 'would know about it, and know about it very fast' (Reuters, Oct 13, 2026).
  • Trump said in July he trusted Beijing and Moscow not to help Tehran in the war (Reuters, Oct 13, 2026).
  • Iran is fortifying its defenses, rallying loyalists, and threatening a wider, harsher response against the US and its Gulf allies as it prepares for possible American attacks after the US midterms, with satellite imagery cited (WSJ via Jerusalem Post, Oct 13, 2026 — reported via one outlet chain, pending wire confirmation).
  • Iran's Supreme National Security Council head said Tehran finds the latest US response 'insufficient' as war-ending negotiations limp along (PBS via Jerusalem Post, Oct 11, 2026 — attributed).
  • German Chancellor Friedrich Merz held a phone call with Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman (Tuesday, Oct 13); the German government said afterward that Iran must engage in serious negotiations and stop destabilising the region (Reuters, Oct 13, 2026).
  • President Trump told reporters at the White House on Monday (Oct 13) that the US midterm elections would not change his Iran policy, that the war would end 'very soon,' and that oil prices would then 'drop like a rock' (Indian Express liveblog, Oct 13, 2026 — attributed, single outlet).
  • MI5 director-general Ken McCallum said Tuesday (Oct 13) that Iran is seeking to intensify attacks in Britain beyond traditional targets toward military sites, and that the suspected RAF Fairford plot 'is not unique' — other examples exist 'not just in the UK but around the world' (Gulf News liveblog, Oct 13, 2026 — attributed, single outlet).

What remains disputed

The scope, success, and casualty toll of the strikes are contested and unverifiable from public sources at build time — no independent casualty figures are included here. The meaning of 'productive discussions' is unstated; it may reflect real back-channel talks or political messaging ahead of the midterms. Iranian claims about Hormuz explosions are unverified.

Why it matters

A war in its eighth month now has a political calendar attached: the US midterms. Trump's stated pause — real or rhetorical — creates a window in which diplomacy (Putin mediation, Gaza ceasefire threads, diesel deals) is suddenly load-bearing. It also sets up a cliff edge: what happens after November 3.

Strategic context

The war began Feb 28, 2026 and has widened across the region: Saudi-led operations in Yemen, Houthi-linked strikes reaching Riyadh, IRGC action in the Strait of Hormuz. Energy markets, shipping insurance, and regional alliances are all downstream of this conflict.

Competing interpretations

Reading one: the pre-midterm pause is genuine — Washington wants de-escalation and is working channels. Reading two: it is electoral management — no new wars before November 3, then reassess. Reading three: Tehran reads the pause as a sign of exhaustion and will use the window to rebuild (see the missile-supply reporting), making the post-midterm cliff sharper.

One year on, Gaza's promised 'day after' has not arrived — AP

A year after the US-brokered ceasefire took effect, all captives are home but Israel controls ~70% of Gaza, reconstruction has not begun, and strikes continue — medical sources report three Palestinians killed Sunday in Khan Younis.

Updated 14 Oct 2026, 06:54 UTC Verified 16 sources
Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/gaza-ceasefire-diplomacy/

What happened

This is a developing-story monitor. The thread: Gaza ceasefire diplomacy continues, with Jared Kushner cautioning that 'hard work' lies ahead. Awaiting verification: the specific contents of the current round of talks, the parties' positions, and any timetable.

What is verified

  • The US-brokered Gaza ceasefire took effect Oct 10, 2025 after mediation by Egypt, Qatar, Turkiye and the US (AP, Oct 11, 2026).
  • All Israeli captives have been returned under the agreement (AP, Oct 11, 2026).
  • Israel now controls ~70% of Gaza; the withdrawal has stalled and partly reversed; reconstruction has not begun (AFP/AP, Oct 11, 2026).
  • 82% of Gaza's standing structures were damaged or destroyed as of mid-2026 (UN, via AFP, Oct 11, 2026).
  • Ali Shaath heads the NCAG; Hamas effectively remains in charge of security (AFP, Oct 11, 2026).
  • Three Palestinians were killed in a Sunday drone strike on a tent in Khan Younis (Nasser Hospital medical source via anews/Anadolu, Oct 11, 2026).
  • Kushner's 'hard work ahead' framing stands (developing diplomacy thread, Oct 10).
  • Israeli strike killed at least 2 Palestinians in southern Gaza on Monday; IDF said it targeted a militant (Reuters wire via Haaretz/TBS News, Oct 12, 2026).
  • Late Sunday airstrike hit a tent in Mawasi, Khan Younis, killing 3; IDF told Reuters it struck militants there (same).
  • Saturday strikes killed at least 5 Palestinians in two attacks (4 near Panda Mall in Gaza City, 1 in Tuffah); IDF said it targeted militants (same).
  • Gaza Health Ministry: 10 killed by Israeli strikes over the preceding 48 hours (9 new + 1 recovered from rubble), 65 wounded (same).
  • Gaza health officials: at least 1,400 Palestinians killed by Israeli fire since the Oct 2025 ceasefire; Israel says 4 soldiers killed by militants (same).
  • Israel says Hamas rearming and launching attacks in violation of the ceasefire; Hamas denies and accuses Israel of undermining the US-brokered deal (same).
  • A joint Israeli military-Shin Bet statement said a wave of strikes over the past 24 hours targeted Hamas facilities: a command center in Daraj Tuffah, a rocket launch site in Al-Furqan, and three weapon depots with rockets, explosives, mortars and rifles (Famagusta Gazette, Oct 12, 2026).
  • The Monday drone strike killed two people (ages 22 and 29) and critically wounded three near the Mu'awiya mosque in al-Mawassi northwest of Rafah; the IDF told Reuters it targeted a 'militant' and results were 'still under review' (L'Orient Today via Wafa/medical sources, Oct 12, 2026).

Russia is supplying missiles to Iran as Tehran rebuilds its arsenal, report says

Iran retains production capacity and substantial ballistic missile stocks despite eight months of US-Israeli airstrikes, according to Bloomberg reporting cited by Haaretz.

Updated 13 Oct 2026, 09:20 UTC Verified 5 sources
Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/russia-iran-missiles-rebuild/

What happened

Russia is supplying Iran with missiles as Tehran rebuilds its arsenal, according to Bloomberg reporting cited by Haaretz on October 10, 2026. The reporting states Iran retains production capacity and substantial ballistic missile stocks despite eight months of US-Israeli airstrikes.

What is verified

  • Bloomberg reporting, cited by Haaretz (Oct 10, 2026), says Russia is supplying missiles to Iran.
  • The reporting states Iran retains ballistic missile production capacity and substantial stocks.
  • US-Israeli airstrikes against Iran have been ongoing for roughly eight months (since Feb 28, 2026).
  • Western intelligence assessed in June that Iran retained ~75% of its prewar arsenal (Bloomberg reporting, carried by Newsmax/NewsBeep, Oct 10, 2026).
  • Britain's navy says at least 10 vessels were struck near Hormuz in the past week; oil above $100/barrel; tanker charters ~$1.2M/day (same).
  • Iran reportedly test-fired multiple ballistic missiles from Tehran and Alborz provinces on Sunday Oct 11 (Türkiye Today, Oct 11, 2026; C14 News, Oct 11, 2026).
  • Bloomberg Economics analysis, carried by Türkiye Today (Oct 11, 2026), says Iran has launched more than 4,000 Shahed drones and at least 2,000 ballistic missiles since Feb 28.
  • Trump said late Monday he does not believe China or Russia are helping Iran 'at all,' telling Truth Social they have 'too much to lose' (Reuters, Oct 13, 2026) — on-record statement contradicting the Bloomberg assessment.

What remains disputed

The types, quantities, and delivery timeline of the missiles have not been publicly specified. The claim that eight months of strikes failed to degrade Iran's missile capability will be contested by US and Israeli officials; no official US/Israeli assessment is reflected in the cited reporting. Russia's strategic intent — defensive resupply vs. deepening war partnership — is unstated.

Why it matters

If accurate, this is the clearest evidence yet that the US-Israeli air campaign has not achieved its stated aim of degrading Iran's strike capability — and that Moscow is willing to backfill it. That combination hardens Iran's negotiating position and lengthens the war's expected runway.

Strategic context

Russia and Iran deepened military cooperation after 2022, most visibly through Iranian drones in Ukraine. The reverse flow — Russian munitions into Iran — marks a new phase. It follows the Putin–Pezeshkian meeting in Ashgabat, where Moscow pledged mediation while visibly tightening its embrace of Tehran.

Competing interpretations

Reading one: this is routine alliance maintenance — Moscow resupplying a partner to keep the war grinding and keep Washington distracted. Reading two: it is a deterrent signal to Washington and Jerusalem ahead of any escalation. Reading three (skeptical): the reporting may overstate deliveries or conflate agreements with delivered hardware; the physical evidence is not public.

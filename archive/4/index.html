IRGC Navy says the Strait of Hormuz is closed again as tanker attacks resume

The strait is shut again after Friday's brief reopening, and will stay that way until the US lifts its blockade of Iranian ports — while a tanker struck by a projectile Saturday night was the first reported attack in more than ten days.

Updated 11 Oct 2026, 19:08 UTC Analysis 24 sources
Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/hormuz-shipping-insurance/

What happened

This is a developing-story monitor. The thread: Iranian media reports of massive explosions in the Strait of Hormuz (cited in Al Jazeera's Oct 9 liveblog) and the reported IRGC strike on the LPG carrier NV Sunshine are the kind of events that move war-risk insurance pricing and tanker routing. Awaiting verification: actual insurance rate moves, rerouting data, and confirmed damage to shipping. Incident volume is now quantified: at least 13 incidents involving oil, LNG and LPG tankers were recorded around the strait between Sept 28 and Oct 5, according to data analyzed by three maritime security sources and cited by Reuters — the highest weekly total since the war began on Feb 28. The UN's International Maritime Organization recorded nine incidents in the same week. The US Navy-led Joint Maritime Information Center said the activity continued into the weekend, demonstrating 'Iran's intent to assert presence along key transit lanes.' Iranian state media reported (Times Now, Oct 10, 23:13 IST) that the IRGC said an unidentified oil tanker exploded and caught fire after hitting a naval mine in the strait. The NV Sunshine strike claim is now contested by the vessel's owner (see st-004). War-risk insurance premium data and confirmed rerouting figures are still not established in this newsroom's sourcing. CENTCOM said Sunday the disabled Ocean Molica/Arika Sun ignored repeated warnings before the stern strike and released footage of the explosion (The National). UKMTO issued a Sunday advisory on an incident involving a tanker and military forces in the Gulf of Oman, details unconfirmed. The IRGC claimed via Sepah News that a 'violating' supertanker exploded after hitting naval mines in the strait — alleged, no independent confirmation. Reuters reports a Tehran–Washington process with proposals to reopen the strait 'within seven days' (via JPost).

What remains disputed

The IMO count (11 tankers, week ending Oct 4) and Kpler's 8.5M bpd flow figure are third-party tracking data, not official government tallies; NY Post's '10 more strikes' for the following week is that outlet's count alone. Iran's claim of 'full control' of the strait sits against the US-escort reality of near-pre-war crude flows — the waterway is contested, not settled by either side's framing. Whether insurers have materially repriced Hormuz risk, whether tankers are rerouting, and the scale of any disruption — all await data. The explosions themselves are reported by Iranian media, not independently confirmed. The naval-mine explosion report comes solely from Iranian state media via the IRGC; the vessel is unidentified and no independent confirmation exists. Whether insurers have materially repriced Hormuz risk remains unestablished — incident counts are not insurance data. A new Oct 11 IRGC claim of striking the LPG carrier NV Sunshine directly conflicts with owner NVTrans's Oct 10 denial that any attack occurred — treat as alleged. The IRGC's naval-mine claim is alleged — only Iranian state media (Sepah News, with Xinhua/IANS carries) reports it; the vessel is unnamed and unidentified, and no independent confirmation exists. Its warning that enforcement will expand beyond Hormuz is escalatory messaging from the same unverified statement, not evidence of capability or intent beyond the words themselves. SNSC Secretary Mohsen Rezaei's claim that the US is 'smuggling' oil through the strait with 'thugs' in Omani waters, involving the UAE military and CENTCOM's commander (state-linked ISNA), is his allegation alone — no evidence publicly offered, no independent confirmation.

Why it matters

Roughly a fifth of global petroleum liquids moves through Hormuz. Even the perception of risk — let alone confirmed strikes on commercial vessels — transmits into oil prices, diesel markets, and the inflation the diesel deal is trying to tame.

Strategic context

The strait has been the Iran war's maritime pressure point since February. Commercial strikes are the sharpest escalation of that pressure: they turn a war between states into a tax on the global economy.

Competing interpretations

Reading one: markets will price this as a contained risk — until a second strike lands. Reading two: insurers move faster than states; a quiet repricing is already underway. Data will decide.

Gaza ceasefire diplomacy at one year — Kushner touts progress, warns 'hard work ahead' on disarmament

One year after the October 9, 2025 agreement, Kushner marked the anniversary with a list of milestones — and conceded the hardest part, Hamas disarmament, is still unfinished.

Updated 11 Oct 2026, 15:20 UTC Analysis 15 sources
Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/gaza-ceasefire-diplomacy/

What happened

October 9, 2026 marked one year since the October 9, 2025 agreement on Trump's 20-point Gaza peace plan. Jared Kushner, in an anniversary post, said 'significant progress' had been made and listed milestones: the October 9, 2025 agreement launching a ceasefire that largely endured; the October 12, 2025 Israeli withdrawal to the 'yellow line'; the return of all 20 living Israeli hostages on October 13, 2025; UN Security Council Resolution 2803 endorsing the plan (Nov 17, 2025); the return of the final deceased hostage's remains in January 2026; the establishment of the Board of Peace, an international stabilization force, and a technocratic Palestinian administration for Gaza. Kushner said Hamas had signed a phased disarmament framework in July 2026 — but acknowledged 'There is still hard work ahead,' saying disarmament 'must be implemented, security established, essential services restored, and communities rebuilt.' Separately, Dawn reported the disarmament track is stalled: Hamas agreed in July to hand weapons to the technocratic government, but Israel objects to the Board of Peace text calling for phased withdrawal and demands 'full demilitarisation' before pulling back; Kushner met Hamas and Israeli leaders in August without breakthrough, both sides holding to their demands. (All milestone claims are Kushner's; the stall assessment is Dawn's.) On Saturday Israeli strikes killed at least five Palestinians in Gaza — four near Panda Mall in Gaza City and one in the Tuffah neighbourhood, per health officials (Dawn, Oct 11). Israel claims Hamas has been rearming and launching attacks in violation of the October ceasefire; Hamas denies it and accuses Israel of undermining the deal. The running tallies: at least 1,400 Palestinians killed in Gaza since the ceasefire per the territory's health officials (Israel says four of its soldiers have been killed); Palestinian authorities say more than 74,000 have been killed since October 2023 (Dawn). Al Jazeera's Oct 10 stock-take of Trump's Board of Peace: the board presented a $2.45 billion recovery plan in September, but reconstruction is stalled; no stabilisation force has deployed — six countries (Indonesia, Morocco, Kazakhstan, Albania, Kosovo and Uganda) have committed personnel toward a 20,000-soldier + 12,000-police force to be headed by CENTCOM; Israel now occupies about 60% of the Strip with the 'Yellow Line' pushed farther in, and Palestinians crossing it unknowingly have been shot dead (Al Jazeera).

What remains disputed

Nearly everything material: who proposed what, whether the gaps are closing, and what 'hard work ahead' refers to in concrete terms. No primary documents or on-record statements beyond the reported Kushner remark have been established here.

Why it matters

Gaza diplomacy and the Iran war are now linked pressure systems. A Gaza ceasefire would change the regional temperature — and the Houthi/Saudi/Yemen escalations show the cost of failure.

Strategic context

Months of on-and-off Gaza negotiations have repeatedly stalled at the same obstacles: hostages, withdrawal terms, and post-war governance. The current round is being watched for whether regional pressure from the Iran war finally forces movement.

Competing interpretations

Reading one: real momentum, with the Iran war creating incentives for a deal. Reading two: managed optics — talks as process, not progress. The evidence is not yet sufficient to choose.

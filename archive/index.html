Trump says US could end Iran war now — but only with guarantees Tehran never goes nuclear

At Joint Base Andrews, Trump said the seven-month war could end immediately with a no-nuclear-weapons guarantee; he claimed 18 US personnel killed and 159 Iranian ships sunk — figures not independently confirmed — and floated renaming the Strait of Hormuz 'the Trump Strait.'

Updated 11 Oct 2026, 19:34 UTC Verified 28 sources
Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/us-israel-iran-war-october/

What happened

Strikes against Iran by the US and Israel began on February 28, 2026, and the conflict continues into October. Al Jazeera's liveblog (Oct 9, 2026) tracks ongoing developments, including Iranian media reports of massive explosions in the Strait of Hormuz. Separately, Trump has said he ruled out an attack on Iran before the November 3 midterm elections, citing 'productive discussions' with Tehran. On October 10, after a second missile attack on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport, US President Trump told reporters the United States 'may' join Saudi Arabia in striking the Houthis. 'We may. We're going to look at it. We just found out about the recent attack. We will make a decision. We move very quickly,' he said outside the White House. Trump also said he would consider restarting US strikes on Iran — despite his earlier public pledge ruling out an attack on Iran before the November 3 midterm elections (Al Jazeera liveblog, New York Post, Times Now, A News, Mathrubhumi, Times of Israel, Oct 10). On October 11, Trump set a public end-condition at Joint Base Andrews after a campaign rally in Tennessee: the US 'could leave right now,' he said, 'but we have to guarantee that they can't have it [a nuclear weapon] in the future. And we'll get that.' He claimed 18 American personnel have been killed in the nearly seven-month conflict, said he had met families of the fallen at Dover Air Force Base, and claimed US operations had sunk 159 Iranian ships since late February and destroyed much of Iran's navy, air force, radar systems and manufacturing infrastructure. He also said Tehran would 'probably do a little bit extra before the election, thinking it's going to hurt us,' and floated renaming the Strait of Hormuz 'the Trump Strait' (IANS, lokmattimes, Oct 11). The casualty count, ship-sinking figure and capability claims are Trump's own assertions and have not been independently confirmed.

What remains disputed

The scope, success, and casualty toll of the strikes are contested and unverifiable from public sources at build time — no independent casualty figures are included here. The meaning of 'productive discussions' is unstated; it may reflect real back-channel talks or political messaging ahead of the midterms. Iranian claims about Hormuz explosions are unverified. The IRGC's new claim of striking the LPG carrier NV Sunshine directly conflicts with owner NVTrans's Oct 10 denial that any attack occurred — treat the strike as alleged. Sen. Murphy's characterization of the blockade's purpose on X is political commentary, not established fact. Trump's Oct 11 figures — 18 US personnel killed, 159 Iranian ships sunk, destruction of Iran's navy/air force/radar/manufacturing — are his claims alone, not confirmed by Pentagon statements or independent evidence in this newsroom's sourcing. Eslami's claim that Iran's nuclear technology 'cannot be halted' is an Iranian-government assertion; the actual state of the program after the strikes is not independently established in this newsroom's sourcing. Ansari's emissions figures are Iran's own measurements. Gharibabadi's framing of US responsibility for the Minab school attack is Iran's claim alone.\n\nThe official DCAS toll (21 dead, 865 wounded) now contradicts the 18-death figure Trump stated publicly on Oct 11; the gap between the Washington Post's September reporting and the Pentagon's earlier database was acknowledged by the Oct 9 correction.

Why it matters

A war in its eighth month now has a political calendar attached: the US midterms. Trump's stated pause — real or rhetorical — creates a window in which diplomacy (Putin mediation, Gaza ceasefire threads, diesel deals) is suddenly load-bearing. It also sets up a cliff edge: what happens after November 3.

Strategic context

The war began Feb 28, 2026 and has widened across the region: Saudi-led operations in Yemen, Houthi-linked strikes reaching Riyadh, IRGC action in the Strait of Hormuz. Energy markets, shipping insurance, and regional alliances are all downstream of this conflict.

Competing interpretations

Reading one: the pre-midterm pause is genuine — Washington wants de-escalation and is working channels. Reading two: it is electoral management — no new wars before November 3, then reassess. Reading three: Tehran reads the pause as a sign of exhaustion and will use the window to rebuild (see the missile-supply reporting), making the post-midterm cliff sharper.

The US-Israel war on Iran: strikes began February 28 — Trump rules out escalation before the midterms

Al Jazeera's live reporting continues to track the war; Trump says he has ruled out a fresh attack before the November 3 midterms while citing 'productive discussions' with Tehran.

Updated 13 Oct 2026, 15:25 UTC Verified 16 sources
Full paper https://times-of-the-world.vercel.app/us-israel-iran-war-october/

What happened

Strikes against Iran by the US and Israel began on February 28, 2026, and the conflict continues into October. Al Jazeera's liveblog (Oct 9, 2026) tracks ongoing developments, including Iranian media reports of massive explosions in the Strait of Hormuz. Separately, Trump has said he ruled out an attack on Iran before the November 3 midterm elections, citing 'productive discussions' with Tehran.

What is verified

  • US-Israeli strikes on Iran began February 28, 2026.
  • Al Jazeera liveblog (Oct 9, 2026) tracks the ongoing war, including Iranian media reports of massive explosions in the Hormuz strait.
  • Trump has publicly ruled out an attack on Iran before the Nov 3 midterms, citing 'productive discussions'.
  • Iran is still reviewing the US response to its proposal on reopening the Strait of Hormuz and will reply to Washington via intermediaries (FM spokesman Baghaei, Reuters, Oct 12, 2026).
  • Message exchange between Iran and the US through mediators is continuing; Qatar and Pakistan among mediators (same).
  • Tehran's seven-point plan unchanged: lift the naval blockade, end the war on all fronts, release frozen Iranian assets (Al Jazeera/Dawn, Oct 12, 2026).
  • Central disagreement: Washington insists on addressing Iran's nuclear programme from the outset; Tehran says talks should focus initially on reopening Hormuz (Reuters, Oct 12, 2026).
  • Iranian FM Abbas Araghchi spoke by phone with Iraqi FM Fuad Hussein on 'the latest regional developments,' including the worsening situation in Yemen (Fuad Hussein Telegram via Al Jazeera liveblog, Oct 12, 2026).
  • Trump said Monday the Nov 3 midterms will have 'no impact' on his Iran approach; the war will end 'very soon' and oil prices will then 'drop like a rock' (Times of Israel liveblog; Indian Express, Oct 12, 2026).
  • Trump said he spoke with Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman about the Houthi strikes: 'The Houthis are misbehaving very badly... No good.' (same).
  • UK PM Andy Burnham told Parliament Monday the RAF Fairford suspected plot was 'highly likely to have been co-ordinated in Iran, almost certainly directed' by the IRGC (AP via TNND; Rudaw; Press Association-syndicated reports, Oct 12, 2026).
  • The Sept 27 Fairford incident involved three suspicious vans; police found gasoline but no explosives; seven men in their 20s arrested, all on bail, none charged — six British nationals and one dual British-Iranian citizen (same).
  • The UK summoned Iran's ambassador and sanctioned two Iranians — IRGC counterespionage chief Ruhollah Bazghandi and Zindashti-network facilitator Shahram Ali Reza Tamarzadeh Jakki — with asset freezes and travel bans (same; Clacton Gazette, Oct 12, 2026).
  • A US State Department joint statement condemned Iran for 'lethal plotting, arson, sabotage, cyber-attacks,' endorsed by more than 20 countries (29 per Indian Express and JFeed, Oct 12, 2026).
  • Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood told MPs a 'known individual based in Iran' was thought to have co-ordinated the plot; Iran denied involvement and asked the UK for evidence (same).
  • The Pentagon confirmed US B-52/B-1 bombers were pulled from RAF Fairford last week and redeployed to home bases (via Reflector/AP, Oct 12, 2026).
  • Trump said late Monday he does not believe China and Russia are helping Iran 'at all,' writing on Truth Social they have 'too much to lose' and that the US 'would know about it, and know about it very fast' (Reuters, Oct 13, 2026).
  • Trump said in July he trusted Beijing and Moscow not to help Tehran in the war (Reuters, Oct 13, 2026).
  • Iran is fortifying its defenses, rallying loyalists, and threatening a wider, harsher response against the US and its Gulf allies as it prepares for possible American attacks after the US midterms, with satellite imagery cited (WSJ via Jerusalem Post, Oct 13, 2026 — reported via one outlet chain, pending wire confirmation).
  • Iran's Supreme National Security Council head said Tehran finds the latest US response 'insufficient' as war-ending negotiations limp along (PBS via Jerusalem Post, Oct 11, 2026 — attributed).
  • German Chancellor Friedrich Merz held a phone call with Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman (Tuesday, Oct 13); the German government said afterward that Iran must engage in serious negotiations and stop destabilising the region (Reuters, Oct 13, 2026).
  • President Trump told reporters at the White House on Monday (Oct 13) that the US midterm elections would not change his Iran policy, that the war would end 'very soon,' and that oil prices would then 'drop like a rock' (Indian Express liveblog, Oct 13, 2026 — attributed, single outlet).
  • MI5 director-general Ken McCallum said Tuesday (Oct 13) that Iran is seeking to intensify attacks in Britain beyond traditional targets toward military sites, and that the suspected RAF Fairford plot 'is not unique' — other examples exist 'not just in the UK but around the world' (Gulf News liveblog, Oct 13, 2026 — attributed, single outlet).

What remains disputed

The scope, success, and casualty toll of the strikes are contested and unverifiable from public sources at build time — no independent casualty figures are included here. The meaning of 'productive discussions' is unstated; it may reflect real back-channel talks or political messaging ahead of the midterms. Iranian claims about Hormuz explosions are unverified.

Why it matters

A war in its eighth month now has a political calendar attached: the US midterms. Trump's stated pause — real or rhetorical — creates a window in which diplomacy (Putin mediation, Gaza ceasefire threads, diesel deals) is suddenly load-bearing. It also sets up a cliff edge: what happens after November 3.

Strategic context

The war began Feb 28, 2026 and has widened across the region: Saudi-led operations in Yemen, Houthi-linked strikes reaching Riyadh, IRGC action in the Strait of Hormuz. Energy markets, shipping insurance, and regional alliances are all downstream of this conflict.

Competing interpretations

Reading one: the pre-midterm pause is genuine — Washington wants de-escalation and is working channels. Reading two: it is electoral management — no new wars before November 3, then reassess. Reading three: Tehran reads the pause as a sign of exhaustion and will use the window to rebuild (see the missile-supply reporting), making the post-midterm cliff sharper.

What changed since last update

TRUMP: WAR WILL END 'VERY SOON', OIL WILL 'DROP LIKE A ROCK' — President Trump told reporters at the White House on Monday that the US midterm elections would not change his Iran policy, that the war would end 'very soon,' and that oil prices would then 'drop like a rock' (Indian Express liveblog, Oct 13 — attributed, single outlet). The remarks sit alongside his Truth Social pledge of no strikes before the November 3 midterms already on the ledger — both on-record projections, neither a verified timetable. MI5 CHIEF ON THE FAIRFORD PLOT — MI5 director-general Ken McCallum said Tuesday that Iran is seeking to intensify its attacks in Britain beyond traditional targets toward military sites, and that the suspected plot to attack RAF Fairford in September 'is not unique' — there have been other examples 'not just in the UK but around the world' (Gulf News liveblog, Oct 13 — attributed, single outlet). The context: Britain has sanctioned two Iranians and summoned Iran's ambassador over the alleged plot; Tehran denies any role (Indian Express liveblog). MERZ–MBS CALL ON IRAN — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz held a phone call with Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman on Tuesday, with Berlin saying afterward that Iran must engage in serious negotiations and stop destabilising the region (Reuters, Oct 13). The call lands on the diplomacy ledger alongside the WSJ-via-JPost report that Iran is fortifying defenses ahead of possible post-midterms US action (v10) — European capitals pressing both de-escalation and Iranian restraint while Gulf infrastructure keeps taking hits. DIGGING IN FOR THE NEXT ROUND — the Jerusalem Post (Oct 13, updated 14:04) carries a Wall Street Journal report that Iran is fortifying its defenses, rallying loyalists, and threatening a wider, harsher response against the US and its Gulf allies as it prepares for potential American attacks after the midterms — seeking to deter them — with satellite imagery indicating defensive preparations (WSJ via JPost, Oct 13 — one reporting chain, kept as reported pending wire confirmation). JPost notes the report sits against a stream of signals that the US or Israel could resume operations: Maariv reported over the weekend that Washington pressed Israel to strike Iran; PBS (Oct 11) quoted Iran's Supreme National Security Council head calling the latest US response 'insufficient' as negotiations limp along; and Netanyahu said in March that Iran could no longer produce ballistic missiles after the earlier strikes — a claim the reported fortification tempo now tests. The ledger keeps the fortification claim and the earlier assessments as claim-vs-claim, not resolved fact. BUSHEHR VICINITY STRUCK TUESDAY — Iran's Atomic Energy Organization said US-Israeli strikes hit the vicinity of the Bushehr nuclear power plant on Tuesday evening, with no technical damage and no human casualties in initial reports (IRNA, Oct 13; via english.newstube.lk). IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi called on X for maximum military restraint to avoid nuclear safety risks during conflict; the IAEA confirmed no rise in radiation levels. Iran's state media says this is the third attack on the Bushehr area this month (via BBC News live recap, Oct 13). ARAK CLAIMED — Israel says it also hit the Arak heavy-water plant in central Iran (Israeli statement, via BBC live recap; single-source claim, kept attributed). TEHRAN AND THE GULF — the BBC live recap reported heavy strikes heard in Tehran in the hours before publication, and the UAE said its air defenses engaged ballistic missiles and drones launched from Iran. Separately, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said via Iranian state media that US-Israeli strikes are targeting civilian sites and warned Iran will exact a heavy price (attributed). Ledger line: the Bushehr vicinity-strike report and the IAEA's no-radiation-rise confirmation are VERIFIED; Iran's civilian-sites framing and Israel's Arak claim remain ATTRIBUTED. OHIO RALLY REMARKS — Speaking at a campaign rally in Youngstown, Ohio, Trump suggested the US "might have gone too far" in targeting Iran's leadership, saying "we're having a hard time figuring out who the hell to deal with, to be honest with you" and "we don't know who the hell we're dealing with any more" — while claiming "nobody wants to be the president of Iran" (TBS News, Oct 13; on-record rally remarks; note Masoud Pezeshkian remains Iran's president). FAIRFORD STATEMENT — The UK government and several allied countries issued a joint statement Monday recognizing Britain's attribution of Iran's involvement in the September 27 incident at RAF Fairford, describing the IRGC's "continued willingness to project hostile activity beyond its borders through covert networks and proxy actors," and warning Iran against further threats; asked about action against Tehran, Trump told reporters "we are going to see" (ANI via Tribune India, Oct 13; allied-government statement, on record). 'NOT AT ALL' — Trump said late Monday he does not believe China and Russia are helping Iran 'at all,' writing on Truth Social they have 'too much to lose' and that the US 'would know about it, and know about it very fast' (Reuters, Oct 13). On-record statement; it cuts against both the Bloomberg Russia-to-Iran missile assessment and reports of Chinese air-defense launcher supplies to Tehran — the ledger keeps it as an on-record presidential claim, not a verified fact. CONTEXT: Trump said in July he trusted Beijing and Moscow not to help Tehran (same). IRGC CHIEF ON HORMUZ — IRGC chief Maj.-Gen. Ahmad Vahidi said that 'no extra-regional power has the right to threaten, maintain a hegemonic presence, or interfere' in the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf (Iranian on-record statement, single-source via Al Jazeera liveblog, Oct 13) — attributed to Tehran; no US response at build time. ARAGHCHI ON DIPLOMACY — FM Abbas Araghchi said diplomatic efforts to achieve Iran's national interests are 'seriously' being pursued at the international level as Tehran seeks to end Western sanctions and strengthen media engagement (on-record, attributed, same source). TRUMP: MIDTERMS 'NO IMPACT' ON IRAN POLICY — asked at the White House Monday whether the November 3 midterms could change his Iran approach, Trump said: 'No, it has no impact.' He repeated that the war 'will be very soon' over and oil prices would then 'drop like a rock' (Times of Israel liveblog; Indian Express, Oct 12). He said he spoke with Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman about the Houthi strikes on Saudi territory: 'The Houthis are misbehaving very badly... No good.' (same). UK ACCUSES IRAN OVER RAF FAIRFORD PLOT — PM Andy Burnham told Parliament Monday that a suspected attack plot targeting RAF Fairford, a US-bomber base in Gloucestershire, was 'highly likely to have been co-ordinated in Iran, almost certainly directed' by the IRGC (AP via TNND; Rudaw; PA-syndicated reports, Oct 12). The Sept 27 incident: three suspicious vans stopped near the base; police found gasoline (no explosives); seven men in their 20s arrested, all released on bail, none charged — six British nationals, one dual British-Iranian (same). The UK summoned Iran's ambassador (who denied involvement and asked for evidence) and sanctioned two Iranians: Ruhollah Bazghandi, the IRGC's head of counterespionage operations, and Shahram Ali Reza Tamarzadeh Jakki, described as a facilitator of the Zindashti criminal network — asset freeze and travel ban (same; Clacton Gazette). A US State Department joint statement condemning Iran for 'lethal plotting, arson, sabotage, cyber-attacks' was endorsed by more than 20 countries — 29 per Indian Express and JFeed. Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood told MPs a 'known individual based in Iran' was thought to have co-ordinated the plot. The Pentagon confirmed US B-52/B-1 bombers were pulled from Fairford last week and redeployed to home bases. Iran unequivocally denied involvement. IRANIAN THREATS, RESTRAINED POSTURE — Iranian military adviser Yahya Rahim Safavi said Tehran aims to 'manage and limit traffic' through the Strait of Hormuz, not close it fully (Indian Express live blog, Oct 12) — on-record but single-source. Separately, SNSC secretary Mohsen Rezaei said regional data cables, data centres and AI facilities could be 'in the line of fire' if the war restarts (Iranian state media via same) — an Iranian-official threat, attributed. DIPLOMATIC CONTACTS — Iranian FM Abbas Araghchi spoke by phone with Iraqi FM Fuad Hussein on 'the latest regional developments,' including the worsening situation in Yemen as the Houthis and Saudi forces trade attacks (Hussein's Telegram channel via Al Jazeera liveblog, Oct 12) — on-record but single-sourced. IRGC CLAIMS THIRD SUPERTANKER HIT — Iran's IRGC Navy said Monday that a third supertanker was hit in the Strait of Hormuz 'this morning,' sparking an onboard fire, claiming the tanker had switched off its navigation system and ignored warnings, 'like the two previous vessels' (Press TV via ANI, carried by Economic Times, Oct 12). The statement did not identify the vessel. Separately, India's NSA Ajit Doval spoke with Iran's SNSC secretary Mohsen Rezaei Monday evening on 'the latest regional and international developments,' per the Embassy of Iran in India (Economic Times, Oct 12) — on-record but single-sourced. TEHRAN REVIEWS THE US RESPONSE (Reuters, Oct 12) — Foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said Monday that Iran is still reviewing the US response to its proposal on reopening the Strait of Hormuz and will reply to Washington via intermediaries when the review is over. Message exchange through mediators continues (Qatar and Pakistan among the mediators). Tehran's seven-point plan (presented at UNGA sidelines) is unchanged: demands include lifting the naval blockade, ending the war on all fronts, and releasing frozen Iranian assets. The central disagreement: Washington insists on addressing Iran's nuclear programme from the outset; Tehran says talks should initially focus on reopening Hormuz. Separately, security chief Mohsen Rezaei said Sunday that Washington accepted some of Tehran's seven conditions but its response was 'inadequate' (Al Jazeera liveblog) — an Iranian-official claim, attributed. Earlier Monday, Baghaei called for Washington to lift its latest sanctions and said the exchange of messages 'is continuing' (Al Jazeera/Dawn). war timeline, Trump's pre-midterm statement, Al Jazeera liveblog status.

What to watch next

  • Whether 'productive discussions' produce anything tangible before Nov 3.
  • Posture of US/Israeli forces after the midterms — the real test of the pause.
  • Hormuz corridor status; any closure threat moves oil markets instantly.
  • Putin's mediation offer and whether Tehran engages.
  • Tehran's reply to Washington via intermediaries once the review is over.
  • Whether the nuclear-programme-vs-Hormuz sequencing gap can be bridged.
  • Independent confirmation of the IRGC's claimed third supertanker strike in Hormuz.
  • Whether UK intelligence releases any evidence behind the 'almost certainly' IRGC assessment.
  • Any Iranian response beyond the denial — escalation or back-channel de-escalation.
  • Whether more countries join the State Department condemnation of Iran.
